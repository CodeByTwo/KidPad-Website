# KidPad — Influencer Outreach & Vetting Kit

A practical kit for partnering with parent/family creators to promote KidPad
(£2.99, one-off, iOS + Android, global). Growth comes from **proactive outreach +
measured results**, not a passive form. Vet before, measure after.

**Golden rule:** never pay on *promised* reach. Give every partner a unique promo
code / tracking link so their real exposure is a number you measured, not a claim.

---

## 1. Who to target (the fit that matters)

**Ideal creator profile:**
- Parents of toddlers/preschoolers (their content is about family life, ages ~1–7).
- **Micro-influencers (5k–50k followers)** are usually the best ROI for a paid app:
  higher engagement, more trust, far cheaper (often just a free code) than big accounts.
- Platforms, in rough priority for this niche: **Instagram** (Reels + Stories),
  **TikTok**, **YouTube** (Shorts or "what's on my kid's iPad" style videos).
- Audience in countries where the app sells — you're global, so this is rarely a blocker,
  but check the audience isn't 90% in a market you don't care about.

**Ignore follower count as your headline metric.** A 15k account with 6% engagement
and a genuinely parent audience beats a 250k account with 0.5% engagement every time.

---

## 2. Where to find them

- Instagram/TikTok hashtag search: `#toddlermum` `#momsofinstagram` `#screentime`
  `#toddleractivities` `#momlife` `#kidsapps` `#gentleparenting` `#preschoolathome`.
- "Best apps for toddlers" roundup videos on TikTok/YouTube — the creators already
  make this exact content.
- Look at who your competitors (other kids' apps) have partnered with — check tagged
  posts and "paid partnership" labels on their profiles.
- Parenting-app review sites and blogs (also a PR/backlink opportunity — see the ASO map).

Build a target list of **10–20** well-fit creators before you start pitching. Quality
of fit beats volume of outreach.

---

## 3. Vetting checklist (score before you commit)

Run each candidate through this. If they can't clear the top three, pass.

- [ ] **Engagement rate** = (avg likes + comments) ÷ followers. Look for **3%+**
      (higher on smaller accounts). Low engagement = dead or bought audience.
- [ ] **Audience is actually parents of little kids** — check comments (real parents
      talking?) and, if they share it, their media-kit audience breakdown.
- [ ] **Geography** overlaps your markets (fine if global; flag if concentrated
      somewhere the app isn't relevant).
- [ ] **Past sponsored posts performed** — do their #ad posts still get real
      engagement, or does it collapse when it's paid? Ask for stats on a recent one.
- [ ] **Content quality & brand fit** — safe, warm, parent-trust tone that matches KidPad.
- [ ] **Red flags:** sudden follower spikes (bought), generic/bot comments ("nice post
      🔥"), engagement rate wildly out of line with follower count, won't share any stats.

**Ask every serious candidate for their media kit** (reach, average views, saves, link
clicks, audience demographics). A creator worth working with has this ready.

---

## 4. Commercial models (and the honest economics)

At £2.99 a download, **a big flat fee rarely pays back on install revenue alone.** Do
the maths: a £300 post needs ~150+ net installs *just to break even* on app revenue
(after store cut). So partner for the *compounding* value too — ratings, reviewable
content you can repost, and the ASO ranking bump from a burst of installs.

| Model | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gifted / free promo code** | Micro-influencers, first partnerships | Cheapest. Give a free code; they post if they genuinely like it. Best ROI early on. |
| **Promo code for their audience** | Any | They share a code/link; you measure installs. Combine with gifting. |
| **Affiliate / rev-share** | Performance-minded creators | Pay per tracked install/sale. Aligns incentives; needs tracking links. |
| **Flat fee** | Proven creators with strong stats | Only once you've *seen* their numbers. Negotiate; rates are soft for unknown apps. |

Start with **gifted codes + a tracking link.** Move to paid only for creators who've
demonstrably driven installs.

## 5. Make it measurable (this is the whole point)

This is how you answer *"what exposure did they actually give us?"* — with data.

**Apple / App Store:**
- **Promo codes** (App Store Connect → your app → Promo Codes): free downloads of the
  paid app, ~100 per version, expire after a set window. Give each creator a batch.
- **Custom Product Pages**: create a variant page with its own URL per creator/campaign;
  App Analytics reports views + conversions per page. Great for attribution.
- **Apple Ads / campaign links** and App Analytics "source" data for click-through.

**Google Play:**
- **Promo codes** (Play Console → Promotions): free/discounted downloads to hand out.
- **Install-referrer / UTM links**: tag each creator's link
  (`?utm_source=<creator>&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=launch`); see them in the
  Play Console acquisition reports.

**Website:** if the creator links to kidpad.app, give them a UTM'd URL too — your GA
already tracks the store-badge clicks (`StoreBadges.tsx`), so you'll see the full
funnel: their traffic → badge click → store.

> ⚠️ Verify current promo-code limits/expiry in each console before you promise codes —
> the platforms change these.

**One code/link per creator, always.** Shared codes make attribution impossible.

## 6. Outreach templates

Keep it short, warm, specific, and lead with *why them*. Personalise the first line —
generic pitches get ignored.

### Cold DM (Instagram/TikTok) — first touch
> Hi [name] — love your [specific thing, e.g. "screen-time balance" / "toddler
> activity ideas] posts. We've just launched **KidPad**, a safe, offline learning app
> for 2–6s — nine pretend-play activities, no ads, no in-app purchases. It feels right
> up your audience's street and I'd love to send you a **free copy** to try with
> [child's name if known / "the little one"], no strings. If you like it, we can chat
> about a partnership. Can I send you a code?

### Email — for creators with a listed business address
> **Subject: KidPad × [name] — a free copy for you (safe kids' app)**
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm [your name] from CodeByTwo, makers of **KidPad** — a safe, offline learning app
> for young children (ages 2–6). Nine colourful pretend-play activities, no ads, no
> accounts, no in-app purchases; a one-off £2.99.
>
> I follow your work because [specific reason], and I think your audience of [parents
> of toddlers] would genuinely get it. I'd love to gift you a copy to try — no
> obligation. If it's a fit, we can talk about a paid or affiliate partnership, and I'd
> share a unique code so we can both see how it lands.
>
> Could I send you a promo code? And if you partner with brands, could you share your
> media kit / typical reach so I can suggest something that works for you both?
>
> Thanks!
> [name] · [email] · kidpad.app

### Follow-up (if no reply after ~5–7 days)
> Hi [name], just floating this back up — still happy to send a free KidPad code your
> way to try, no strings. Totally understand if it's not for you!

## 7. What to give a confirmed partner (the brief)

Send a short, friendly brief — don't over-script it; authentic beats corporate:
- **Their unique promo code + tracking link** (Apple + Google as relevant).
- 2–3 **key points** to mention: safe & offline, no ads/IAP, nine activities, ages 2–6.
- A few **screenshots / a short screen-recording** they can use (yours are in `public/`).
- **Disclosure requirement (non-negotiable):** they must clearly label it as an ad —
  `#ad` / "paid partnership". UK: ASA rules. US: FTC. This protects you and them, and
  it's especially important for parent/kids content.
- A soft **posting window** (not a rigid script — let them sound like themselves).

---

## 8. Simple tracking sheet

One row per partnership so you learn what works. Suggested columns:

| Creator | Platform | Followers | Eng. % | Deal (gift/code/fee) | Cost | Code/UTM | Posted | Installs (tracked) | Rating bump | Cost/install | Worth repeating? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

After a few partnerships, `cost ÷ tracked installs` tells you which creator *types* pay
off — then double down on those.

---

## 9. Workflow summary

1. Build a target list of 10–20 well-fit creators (§1–2).
2. Vet each against the checklist; drop anyone who can't clear the top three (§3).
3. Reach out — gift a free code first (§6).
4. For keen creators, agree a model and issue a **unique code + tracking link** (§4–5).
5. Send the brief with disclosure requirement (§7).
6. Log results in the sheet; measure cost-per-install; repeat what works (§8).

**Do NOT** pay a flat fee before you've seen a creator's real stats or driven measured
installs. Start cheap and code-based; scale spend only toward what's proven.
